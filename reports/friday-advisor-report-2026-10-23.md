# ERBOT Blocking Experiments — Full-Protocol (v2) Results

**Prepared for the Friday meeting, October 23, 2026**
**Status:** Full protocol complete. E1 (collective blocking) and E2 (marginal-allocation blocking) have now been run under the v2 harness — exact budget alignment, permutation attribution tests, pre-registered kill lines — on three datasets (Cora, D10K, MusicBrainz-20K). Headline: **the marginal-allocation effect is real and permutation-certified on Cora, the community-structure explanation is rejected by our own test, and the two larger datasets define the effect's scope: it exists exactly where the fixed-k baseline cannot saturate.**

---

## 1. Summary

The October 16 design reframed blocking as a budget-allocation problem and pre-registered two experiments with kill lines. The prototypes produced one large effect (E2 on Cora, dPC = +0.3227) and one weak positive (E1, +0.0248) with two open caveats. The v2 full protocol was built to settle attribution before any claim is made: budgets aligned to exactly B pairs on both sides, permutation tests (R = 199) that destroy the claimed mechanism while preserving everything else, and two additional datasets.

Three results came back:

1. **E2's effect survives every control on Cora** — and the permutation test decomposes it honestly: roughly half of the +0.3227 is generic pool mechanics (any reallocation of the same budget profile gains ≈ +0.17), and the other half is the marginal-value allocation itself (observed beats all 199 permuted profiles, p = 0.005, for both calibration variants).
2. **E1's headline grew but its explanation died.** At exact budget alignment E1's gain on Cora is +0.2835 — yet the community-label permutation reproduces it exactly (null = observed, sd = 0, p = 1.0). The gain is produced by global top-B selection from the candidate pool during budget alignment, not by community structure. E1 and E2's Cora gains are the same mechanism — allocation — seen twice.
3. **D10K and MusicBrainz-20K bound the claim.** On D10K the baseline is literally perfect (PC = 1.0000); on MusicBrainz-20K it is at 0.995. Every kill line fired there, as pre-registered. The allocation effect is a property of the low-signal regime — which is where blocking is actually hard.

We would rather bring you this version — one certified effect, one rejected explanation, one scope law — than three uncorrected headlines.

## 2. Protocol (v2, changes from the prototypes)

- **Budget alignment, two-sided:** treatments are trimmed *and topped up* to exactly the baseline's pair count B (prototype E1 trimmed only and ran 12.8% under budget, confounding its headline).
- **Permutation attribution tests (R = 199):** for E1, community labels are shuffled (community sizes preserved, identity destroyed); for E2, the per-record allocation profile is shuffled (budget distribution preserved, assignment destroyed). A claim survives only if the observed dPC beats the permuted null.
- **Kill lines, pre-registered and unedited:** a treatment is "dead on this dataset" if dPC ≤ 0, and an attribution is rejected if permutation p ≥ 0.05. An exact tie (dPC = +0.0000) counts as dead; we report the one borderline footnote rather than rewording the line.
- **Discipline unchanged:** entity-disjoint 70/30 splits; hyperparameters (k*, resolution, calibration) tuned on training entities only; test set reported once.
- **Datasets and gold:** Cora (bibliographic; E1 test 729 records, E2 test 604). D10K (n = 10,000, 6,000 entities, 8,705 gold pairs; the synthetic Dirty-ER dataset of the JedAI benchmark — census-style person profiles generated by Febrl from real name/address frequency tables with up to 10 realistic error modifications per entity, 40% of records duplicates; Papadakis et al., Information Systems 93 (2020), Table 1. Febrl is Christen's record-linkage system, but its generator emits a single originals-plus-duplicates table and JedAI distributes the result as a Dirty-ER (deduplication) benchmark — one collection, no source split; our copy has the same single-table layout, so the deduplication reading is the dataset's own, not our reinterpretation. Gold pair file verified complete on 2026-10-21: its 8,705 listed pairs are exactly the 8,705 true pairs under union-find, zero rows dropped; single rich "Aggregate Value" field — all attributes flattened into one space-separated string, the original 12-field structure not preserved in this distribution; near-identical synthetic duplicates. The raw text was also inspected directly: ordinary person-attribute values only, no record ids and no cluster-signature tokens in the text). MusicBrainz-20K: a whole-cluster sample (seed 20261020) of the Leipzig MusicBrainz file — the 20M variant (19,375,000 records, 10,000,000 CID clusters, per the Leipzig benchmark table), not the 20K release; gold = CID. Sample: 20,000 records, 10,294 entities, 16,934 matched pairs.

## 4. Findings

### 4.1 E2 on Cora: the allocation effect, decomposed

Baseline (fixed k = 50): 20,898 pairs, PC = 0.5865. Marginal allocation: 20,575 pairs (1.5% *under* budget), PC = 0.9092 for both variants — the unlabeled variant needs no gold labels to reach the same allocation, which matters for applicability. The mechanism is visible in the budget diagnostics: 56% of records receive fewer pairs than the fixed k (baseline: 0%), i.e., budget measurably flows from easy records to hard ones.

The permutation test is what makes this reportable. Shuffling *who gets* the budget while keeping the profile's shape yields null dPC = +0.170 ± 0.016: about half of the headline is available to any reallocation, through the pool mechanics (top-up and de-duplication). The observed +0.3227 exceeds all 199 shuffles (empirical p = 0.005, the floor at R = 199). The precise claim is therefore: **who gets the budget matters, beyond the shape of the budget profile** — and that increment, ≈ +0.15 PC, is the contribution, stated at its true size rather than the headline size.

### 4.2 E1 on Cora: a larger headline, a rejected explanation

At exact alignment (24,267 pairs both arms), E1's treatment reaches PC = 0.9885 against the baseline's 0.7050 — dPC = +0.2835, an order of magnitude above the prototype's unaligned +0.0248. The end-to-end anomaly from the prototype is also resolved: the two candidate sets genuinely differ (pair-set Jaccard = 0.516) but share a strong-edge skeleton, so Leiden at resolution 1 converges to identical partitions (571 clusters in both arms). Not a bug.

But the community-label permutation returns null PC = 0.9885 with sd = 0.0000 — the observed result is reproduced *exactly* when community identity is destroyed. Reading the alignment code shows why: two-sided alignment trims low-weight pairs and pads from a label-independent top-k pool, so the aligned set is the pool's global top-B regardless of labels. **The +0.2835 cannot be attributed to community structure, and we do not claim it.** One design gap remains: v2 did not log the native (pre-alignment) pair count or native-only PC, so the credit split inside the observed run is unknown; §7 proposes the cheap decisive check. Either way, E1's Cora gain is fully consistent with the allocation mechanism that E2 isolates directly — the unifying hypothesis is that there is one effect here, not two.

### 4.3 D10K and MusicBrainz-20K: the scope condition

On D10K the fixed-k baseline already achieves PC = 1.0000 (E1 at aligned budget: treatment 0.9992, dPC = −0.0008, ≈ 1 true pair — noise against a perfect baseline). E2 variant A ties the baseline exactly (PC = 1.0000) using 1.3% fewer pairs (99,843 vs 101,201); per the pre-registered wording the tie is recorded as dead, with this efficiency observation kept as a footnote, not a claim. Variant B: dPC = −0.0004. Permutation tests are uninformative at the ceiling, as expected.

MusicBrainz-20K is the same picture one notch down (baseline PC ≈ 0.995, ≈ 0.5% headroom): E1 dPC = −0.0025 (≈ 13 true pairs — at near-saturation, reallocation can only swap away pairs the per-record top-k already holds). E2: A dPC = −0.0010, B dPC = −0.0026, both dead by the kill line. One nuance is worth reporting because it sharpens the scope claim: variant A's observed dPC (−0.0010) is significantly *less negative* than its random-profile null (−0.0060 ± 0.0012, p = 0.005). The ordering "learned allocation beats random allocation" therefore holds on both Cora (null +0.170 vs observed +0.3227) and MusicBrainz (null −0.0060 vs observed −0.0010); what changes sign across datasets is not the quality of the allocation but the headroom above the baseline. Variant B shows no such signal on MusicBrainz (observed inside its null, p = 0.93).

### 4.4 The pattern across datasets

Read together, the three columns are one empirical statement: **the marginal-allocation gain tracks the baseline's headroom.** Where per-record kNN is weak (Cora, baseline PC 0.59–0.71 — sparse, ambiguous records), reallocating the same budget by marginal value buys a large, permutation-certified improvement. Where kNN saturates (PC ≥ 0.995), the budget question has no room to operate and no allocation scheme can win. This scope law is the paper's central claim; it also tells us where the method should be evaluated and deployed — large, noisy, low-signal collections, not clean benchmarks — and it was obtained by letting the kill lines fire, not by selecting datasets post hoc.

## 5. Limitations

- **End-to-end clustering collapses on two datasets.** On D10K and MusicBrainz-20K, `er_cluster` returns singletons in *both* arms (e.g., D10K: 3,009 clusters = test rows, ARI = 0), while on Cora it produces 571 clusters. The collapse is identical across arms, so the blocking comparisons are unaffected, but end-to-end claims for those datasets are blocked until the clustering layer is addressed; we report it as a limitation rather than letting it silently cap the pipeline.
- **Permutation resolution.** p = 0.005 is the floor at R = 199. The Cora E2 result beat all 199 shuffles for both variants, but a publication-grade p-value needs a larger R for the headline test.
- **E1 attribution gap.** v2 did not log native-only (pre-alignment) statistics, so E1's observed gain cannot be decomposed further from existing output (§7, item 1).
- **MusicBrainz numbers describe a 20K cluster sample**, drawn whole-cluster from a 19.4M-record file; they are not full-file results. The full file is, however, now a local scale asset with gold labels (§7, item 3).
- **Single split per dataset.** All splits are entity-disjoint 70/30 with tuning confined to the training side; multi-seed replication is still owed for the paper version.

## 6. What this means for the paper

The claim we can defend: *blocking is a budget-allocation problem; allocating a fixed pair budget by marginal expected recall yields a large, permutation-certified completeness gain exactly in the regime where blocking is hard (weak per-record signal), and no gain where the baseline saturates.* The rejected community attribution strengthens the paper rather than weakening it: the most plausible competing explanation for the Cora gains was tested inside our own design and eliminated, leaving allocation as the single mechanism consistent with all six cells of the matrix. The unlabeled calibration variant reaching the identical allocation on Cora removes the "needs gold to tune" objection for the flagship result.

## 7. Proposed next steps (for discussion)

1. **E1 v3 attribution check (cheap, decisive):** log native pair count and native-only PC before alignment, and a variant that pads only from within-community candidates. This settles whether community structure contributes *anything* once allocation is controlled — either a small real effect or a clean negative for the paper.
2. **E3 — exclusion blocking with budget recycling** (the pipeline's second stage, pre-registered October 16): pair-level conflict predicates remove high-confidence non-duplicates and recycle the freed budget through marginal allocation. Cora is the right testbed — it is where headroom exists.
3. **Scale track:** a design and dataset card are ready for North Carolina voter-registration data (gold = NCID; staged ladder 100K → full file, each stage with its own kill line). The full 19.4M-record MusicBrainz file is a second, already-local scale ladder. Either tests whether the Cora regime (weak baseline) reappears at scale.
4. **E4 — gold-free blocking audit** (risk-limiting-audit adaptation) after E3, so the pipeline can eventually report PC with confidence intervals on unlabeled data.

Our suggested priority is 1 → 2 → 3; we would value your view on whether the scale evidence (3) should instead precede E3.
